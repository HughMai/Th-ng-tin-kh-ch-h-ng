## How To Get Customers So Fast It Feels ILLEGAL
`https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FMzKk73iUhw`

- **Core idea:** You don't get more customers by spending more — you get them by building a business that can *afford to* spend more (higher LTV) and by removing every reason a prospect has to hesitate. Speed of customer acquisition is an output of offer + LTV + trust, not budget.

- **Key frameworks:**
  - **Give-away-the-paid-thing** — find what competitors charge for, calc your hard cost to deliver it, give it away free, measure leads → conversions. Branding at scale = pre-conquering "need-to-believes" before purchase.
  - **"What would it take to be #1?"** — replaces incremental thinking (10% better) with goal-backward reverse-engineering from the #1 outcome. Usually you already have the resources; you just haven't pointed them there.
  - **Sell in a vacuum** — eliminate price competition two ways: (1) sell *where* no one else sells (small pond, no other fishermen), or (2) sell *what* no one else can (unique feature/tech).
  - **Pre > Post** ("an ounce of pre is worth a pound of post") — front-load thinking (the idea/hook/concept) where leverage is highest and cost is lowest, instead of fixing things after you hit publish.
  - **Need-to-believes** — minimize the number of things a prospect must believe to buy. Amazon/Uber win by reducing it to a "half-belief." Multiple "humps" = hard sale.
  - **Split-test priority stack** — (1) Offers, (2) Packaging (headlines/sub-headlines), (3) Images, (4) Third-party integrations (the sleeper — biggest wins, hardest to do). Lifts are permanent: build once, gain ongoing.
  - **LTV is the arms race of advertising** — ad costs only ever rise; LTV must rise faster. "Leads cost too much" is almost never the real problem — not making enough per customer is.
  - **Affiliates done right ("so good it hurts")** — don't offer 20% commission. Peel off one component of your product, let the partner sell it and keep 100% of that revenue (set a price floor so leads stay qualified). You pay only hard cost for a pre-paid, pre-qualified lead.
  - **Truth as the ultimate ad** — "nothing sells like the truth." Damaging-admission formula: **[bad] BUT [good]** — claim your flaw first so no one can hold it against you, and it amplifies the good (the Eminem/8-Mile move).

- **Tactical takeaways:**
  1. List what local competitors charge for, pick one cheap-to-deliver thing, give it away free as your lead magnet — measure leads and conversion.
  2. Before optimizing anything, Google "average cost to acquire customer [your industry]" — if yours is ~industry-average, your business (margin/LTV), not your ads, is the constraint.
  3. Cut "need-to-believes" to one (ideally zero). If your offer requires convincing someone of two separate things, split it into a multi-step campaign.
  4. Spend your time in pre-production (the hook, the concept, the offer), not editing/fixing afterward.
  5. Build an affiliate offer where the partner keeps 100% of a peeled-off component — you absorb only hard cost for a qualified, pre-paid lead.
  6. Put your weakness in the ad copy: "[honest flaw] BUT [strong benefit]."

- **Best quote:** *"Leads costing too much is rarely ever an issue. The issue is usually that you don't make enough money per customer."*

- **Applies to Wollongong trades:** Speed-to-lead is exactly this video's thesis — the "every 6 seconds we get a lead" is the product you're selling. For your electrician pitch: (1) the missed-call-text-back automation IS the free give-away that competitors can't match; (2) "what would it take to be #1 for emergency electrical lead response in Wollongong?" → instant 60-second callback; (3) need-to-believe for a tradie is just "a missed call = a lost job" — one belief, easy sale; (4) affiliate play: partner with non-competing trades (plumbers, builders) who refer and keep a cut. Don't compete on price — sell in the vacuum of *speed* no local competitor offers.

## Watch This To Generate 1000s of Leads (In Any Niche)
`https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Mst4hreQYl0`

- **Core idea:** Stop sending cold traffic straight to "book a call / get a quote" — most visitors aren't ready to buy and they leave forever. Insert a **lead magnet**: a free/cheap mini-offer that completely solves a *narrow* problem, which then reveals the bigger problem your core offer solves. Same traffic, 3–5× more leads, drops straight to the bottom line.

- **Key frameworks:**
  - **Mini-offer logic** — solve a narrow problem completely; that solution exposes the next problem (your paid offer). "When someone pays with time now, they're more likely to pay with money later."
  - **3 types of lead magnet** — (1) **Reveal a problem** (assessment/audit that creates deprivation, esp. problems that worsen while waiting), (2) **Free trial** (taste-test; give the solution then remove it to create deprivation), (3) **One step of a multi-step process** (first of many; worthless alone, so they need the rest).
  - **4 delivery formats** — Software/tools, Information (mini-courses/guides — infinitely scalable, zero op-drag), Services (do work free *for qualified leads only*), Physical products. Combine freely.
  - **Sell at the point of greatest deprivation** — don't sell water right after they've drunk; sell the next need (dessert after the entrée). Your paid offer should satisfy a *different, more expensive* need than the freebie.
  - **Name it right (most slept-on lever)** — packaging/headline changes opt-in rate 2–10× more than the contents. Conventions: *Number + Outcome + Timeframe*; *How to X without [bad thing]*; *The [adjective] [thing] that [result]*; *X Mistakes that [prevent good thing]*. Test by asking your audience.
  - **CTA formula** — Clear (not clever) + exact next action + reason to act now. Ask *often*; sales reps who ask most close most. Any reason beats no reason ("because I have a dog" still worked).
  - **Qualify to kill the freebie-seeker objection** — add a dropdown/qualifier; route qualified leads to the good offer, others elsewhere.

- **Tactical takeaways:**
  1. Replace "get a quote" as the *only* CTA with a free mini-offer (e.g. a free assessment/audit).
  2. Build a "reveal a problem" magnet — free inspection/audit that quantifies the cost of inaction, ideally a problem that worsens over time (builds urgency).
  3. Pick a delivery format you can afford: information costs nothing; free service to *qualified* leads only.
  4. Spend two days split-testing the *name* before launching — outcome-led, not vehicle-led ("big booty boot camp," not "6-week squat seminar").
  5. Add CTAs before, inside, and after the magnet; always attach a reason-to-act-now (scarcity from your real capacity limit).
  6. Do the CAC math: if free thing costs $25 hard cost and 1-in-4 buy, CAC = $100 — fine if customer value ≫ $100.

- **Best quote:** *"When someone pays with time now, they're more likely to pay with money later... bad lead magnets don't work. The problem is when you're starting out, you just don't know that you suck."*

- **Applies to Wollongong trades:** The lead magnet for electricians writes itself — **"Free 15-point home electrical safety check"** (reveal-a-problem type; switchboard, smoke alarms, RCD test). It quantifies risk that *worsens while waiting* (urgency), it's a service you deliver to *qualified* homeowners only, and it exposes the paid job (rewire/switchboard upgrade). Name-test it: "Free Safety Check" vs "Is Your Switchboard a Fire Risk? Free Check." Pair with your speed-to-lead system: the instant text-back *delivers* the magnet offer in <60s. This is the front-end you can sell a tradie before they'll trust you with their lead flow.

## How to Get Your Customers to Stay FOREVER
`https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-j8_YCWZ05Q`

- **Core idea:** Retention is the highest-ROI lever in any recurring-revenue business — cutting churn from 20% to 10% *doubles* LTV. Growth isn't a steep new-sales curve; it's a flat retention curve that compounds. Win by *subtraction* (remove friction/overwhelm), not addition.

- **Key frameworks:**
  - **Leaky bucket** — MRR grows only when joins > cancels. Track joins vs cancels monthly; label the business growing/flat/declining.
  - **Retention benchmarks** (online/recurring services) — 80% monthly retention = average; <10% churn = best; <70% retention = fix this first.
  - **Churn drops with time (cohorts)** — months 1–3 are highest churn (20%+); past day 90 it halves (~10%); past month 6 it craters (~2%). Don't ask "how do I lower churn?" — ask "how do I get them past day 90 / month 6?"
  - **Ask why they leave / why they stay** — tag cancel reasons (price / overwhelm / not using / missing feature / not as expected) in a sheet; review top 1–2 per 20 cancels and fix. Ask longest-tenured members what made them stay → that's your retention engine.
  - **Fix by subtraction** — fulfill the original promise and remove friction; don't add features. (Planet Fitness stripped to what 85% actually used and won.)
  - **Overwhelm is the #1 churn driver after price** — "5 features but use 1" feels like 20% value; one feature at same price feels like 100%. One community deleted content → churn 30%→5% (6× LTV). Ask: "If I deleted everything, what one thing would you fight for?"
  - **Tier on evidence, not guesses** — if *ideal* customers say "too expensive," add a lower tier; if *non-ideal* people say it, price is a good filter — ignore them.
  - **Move the ladder to the front** — front-load the stickiest thing into onboarding; deliver a fast win in the first 24h.
  - **Show up daily & cut the cancer** — owner daily presence reliably lifts retention; delete bad content/people.
  - **Annual plan** (buy-10-get-2, ~16%) — one decision vs twelve; annual buyers churn less and are more committed.
  - **Belonging via 10 regulars + one friend** — one friend in a group → 5× longer tenure; matchmake new members to your top 10.

- **Tactical takeaways:**
  1. Track joins vs cancels monthly; benchmark against 80% retention.
  2. Build a specific play for each cliff: month 1 = onboarding/expectations; day 90 = deliver an outcome; month 6 = connect them to others.
  3. DM every canceller, tag the reason, fix the top patterns.
  4. Audit your offer for overwhelm — ask "what one thing would you fight to keep?" and cut the rest.
  5. Front-load a 24-hour fast win; show up daily.
  6. Add an annual plan today (~16% off).

- **Best quote:** *"Most people think growth looks like [a steep climb], when in fact growth really just looks like a flat retention curve — and then it just keeps growing."*

- **Applies to Wollongong trades:** Your business model is the recurring-revenue case — you're selling tradies a *monthly* speed-to-lead service, so *their* churn is your survival. Map the cliffs: month 1 = onboard fast, show booked jobs from missed calls (the fast win in <24h); day 90 = prove pipeline ROI with a simple "jobs won via callback" number; month 6 = make the dashboard a daily habit. Beat overwhelm by selling *one* thing that obviously works (every missed call → instant text-back → booked job), not a bloated dashboard. Offer an annual plan once you've proven it. And "show up daily" = a weekly ROI summary text to the tradie so they feel you caring.

## 10x Your Profit. 10x Your Pricing. 10x Your Business. [Here's how]
`https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9unucIBuNio`

- **Core idea:** Pricing power comes from *branding*, and branding is just **the association between two things, built by repetition**. Pair your unknown thing (you/your business) with something the audience already knows and *likes*, over and over, until the liking transfers. A strong brand lets you charge 10× more, convert 10× higher, and starve competitors — the "100× differential" that prints money.

- **Key frameworks:**
  - **Branding = pairing** — repeatedly associate yourself with things your ideal audience likes; eventually you can remove the prop and the association stays. A chaotic brand (mixed associations) means nothing to nobody — prune the garden.
  - **Why it matters (CAC ↔ LTV)** — every business reduces to "how much does it cost to make money." Brand lowers CAC, raises conversion %, raises price tolerance, and drives repeat/exclusive buying — the four most valuable economic effects.
  - **The WHO (4 qualifiers)** — pick an audience that is (1) **growing**, (2) **can afford** you, (3) **easy to target**, (4) **in pain**. ($100M Offers framework.)
  - **The WHAT** — list what that who *likes* and *struggles with*; that's your content map.
  - **3 levels of belief** — (1) what *you* tell them (weakest; a movie trailer), (2) what *others* tell them (word of mouth; far stronger — the "Maverick" effect), (3) what *they experience* (strongest). Branding climbs this ladder.
  - **Advertising ≠ branding** — advertising is the *process of making known*; branding is *what* you make known. You can have great advertising + bad branding (Bud Light/Dylan Mulvaney for that audience).
  - **Virtuous cycle of branding** — make a promise → keep it → they tell others → others try → repeat. The #1 step is *keeping promises* (product delivers what you said, how you said, when you said).
  - **One central pillar** — keep one core thread (for Hormozi: business-first); you can sprinkle identity around it but the pillar must stay first or you lose the common thread.

- **Tactical takeaways:**
  1. Define your WHO against the four qualifiers before making any content.
  2. List everything that WHO likes / struggles with → that list IS your content calendar.
  3. Make content that keeps a *micro-promise* (teaches one useful thing) so they believe your *macro-promise*.
  4. Engineer word-of-mouth (level 2) — it beats anything you say about yourself.
  5. Protect the central pillar; every association you add trades some audience for more of the who you want — make sure the trade nets the right people.
  6. Above all, keep promises — deliver exactly what you sold; that's what compounds the brand.

- **Best quote:** *"What other people say to them matters way more than what you tell them."*

- **Applies to Wollongong trades:** Your WHO is dialed — Wollongong emergency-trade SMBs (growing demand, can afford a recurring fee, easy to find via local directories/FB groups, in acute pain from missed jobs). Your WHAT: they like more booked jobs, less admin, no missed calls. Build the brand by *pairing yourself with "the speed-to-lead guy for Wollongong trades"* — post case studies ("electrician booked a $4k job from a missed call we caught in 60s"). That's level-2 proof (others talking), which lets you charge a premium instead of competing on price. Keep the promise on your first client and word-of-mouth becomes your whole sales engine. One pillar: speed-to-lead for trades — don't dilute into "general AI automation."

## After Closing 4000+ Sales, I Discovered a New Method to Close Deals Faster
`https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVbvhPGFi6E`

- **Core idea:** The skill that separates top closers is **reframing** — the 1–3 sentences you say after a prospect says anything but "yes" that keep rapport intact so you can keep asking. The person asking the questions controls the conversation; never let the prospect become judge, jury, and executioner of your answers.

- **Key frameworks:**
  - **3A reframe** — **Acknowledge** (say their concern back; buys thinking time, signals listening) → **Associate** (link their question to what your *best customers* ask/do) → **Ask** (question their question instead of answering it).
  - **Rule 1 — Don't answer, ask about the question.** Prospects believe almost nothing you say and almost everything they say. Breadcrumb them to conclude *themselves* that they're a good fit. Never ask "Do you have any questions?" (hands them the wheel).
  - **Rule 2 — "Be like smoke."** No one can disagree with a question. Never argue; you never win a sale by winning an argument.
  - **Rule 3 — Tell them what their question means.** Relabel a step-away as a buying signal ("90% of clients who switch already had an advisor — means you're up the learning curve").
  - **Rule 4 — Straw men for hard truths.** Deliver tough messages via a third party so you don't insult the prospect: (1) "someone earlier today asked the same thing…", (2) reference a successful past customer ("just like Sarah, who runs a plumbing business…"), (3) appeal to authority ("Alex told me this…").
  - **Rule 5 — Childlike curiosity.** "Huh, that's interesting — can I ask more about that?" Seek to understand, not to win. Keep the human #1.
  - **Ethics gate** — only valid if the prospect is *qualified* and you *state facts / tell the truth*. Then you're obligated to ask as many times as needed.
  - **Go-to questions:** "What's your main concern?" / "What are you most afraid of?" / "What would make this a no?" / "What would it take for you to say yes?"

- **Tactical takeaways:**
  1. When asked a trap question ("how many certifications…?"), ask about the question ("which are you looking for specifically, and why?").
  2. On "I need to think about it" → "Totally fair — what are the main variables you're weighing?"
  3. On "I need to talk to my spouse" → "Sensible — what parts do you think they'd push back on?"
  4. Never answer from a weaker knowledge position — use the surgeon/mechanic analogy ("I can't diagnose without looking under the hood").
  5. Pre-plant labels ("you seem like a smart, honest decision-maker") and cash them in at the close.
  6. Practice until no question surprises you — you've had 1000 of these conversations; they've had one.

- **Best quote:** *"You don't close sales by being right — you close sales by making the prospect right."*

- **Applies to Wollongong trades:** Hormozi literally uses *a 22-year-old selling to a 45-year-old $10M plumber* as his example — that's you. Your age means the "put my coach hat on" move won't land; lean on **straw men** instead ("a sparky in Wollongong asked me the exact same thing last week — here's what happened when he tried it"). When a tradie says "I don't have time to learn another system," don't argue — ask "what would make this worth your time?" and reframe to the missed-job cost. Qualify hard first (do they actually miss calls / lose jobs?) so you keep the ethical high ground and can ask for the close as many times as it takes.

## 7 Ways To Get Customers for Free
`https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qpQvdBFW_yI`

- **Core idea:** Referrals are the cheapest, highest-quality customers — they close at higher rates, pay more, stay longer, and refer again. ~30% of business can come from referrals *at any scale*, and that percentage offsetting churn sets your at-rest growth rate. Most people just never *ask properly*.

- **Key frameworks:**
  - **Referral % vs churn % = growth rate** — 20% referrals against 10% churn → you grow 2:1 forever, free.
  - **"Who else?" not "anyone?"** — ask "who else do you know who'd benefit?" (a problem-solving question), never "do you know anybody?" (a yes/no that gets "no").
  - **1. Gift card play** — gift cards valued at one core service unit, 90%+ discount, limit 2 per customer (forces buying 2 → 2 referral leads, and you *capture their names*). 30-day expiry for urgency. You get *paid* to acquire leads, then upsell the gift card against a 10× package.
  - **2. Discount-for-referrals** — when they ask for a discount: "Sure — give me three friends' intros right now." Never discount without a change in terms. Discount ≈ your normal CAC.
  - **3. Bonus instead of discount** — offer $400 of *service* (costs you $80) rather than $200 off (costs you $200 cash); higher perceived value, lower real cost, more usage.
  - **4. "Want to know how to get this free?"** — post-sale, offer cash per referral ($100/head). Drove 30% of sales.
  - **5. Referral at success** — at the transformation moment, take a selfie on *their* phone and three-way text it to people they know ("this is Sandra, you should meet Alex").
  - **6. Handwritten-card event** — invite customers to a quarterly appreciation event; PS: "screenshot this and text a friend — they get in too." 30–50% of the room becomes plus-one leads. Set follow-up appointments, don't sell there.
  - **7. Spouse request** — pre-empt the obstacle: "people who do this with their spouse are 3× more successful" — bring the partner.
  - **Bonus — "bring a friend"** — just say it in every piece of content/ad; one operator got 25–30% unattributed word-of-mouth from this alone.

- **Tactical takeaways:**
  1. Bake "who *else* do you know who'd want this?" into every close.
  2. Run a holiday gift-card promo: 1 service-unit value, 90% off, max 2, 30-day expiry — capture both recipient names.
  3. Never give a naked discount — trade it for 3 intros made *on the spot*, or flip it to a service bonus.
  4. Capture referrals at the peak success moment via a selfie + three-way text from their phone.
  5. Host a low-budget quarterly client event with a "screenshot-to-invite-a-friend" PS.

- **Best quote:** *"Everybody wants to get customers, but ain't nobody want to ask for referrals."*

- **Applies to Wollongong trades:** Referrals are gold in trades — tradies and homeowners talk. For *your* SaaS sale to electricians: after onboarding a sparky and showing them their first recovered job, hit the **referral-at-success** moment — "who else do you know running a trade who's losing jobs to missed calls?" and three-way text the intro. The **bring-a-friend** mechanic scales your outreach for free in tight local trade networks. And build referral capture *into the product you sell them* — your speed-to-lead tool can auto-text their happy customers "know anyone else who needs a sparky?" — making referrals a feature, not a favour.

## Make More Profit than 99% of People
`https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=41EvCgwPrDc`

- **Core idea:** Pricing is the single strongest profit lever — ~6× stronger than acquiring more customers and ~2–3× stronger than cutting churn or increasing purchase frequency. Most pricing questions are *math problems*, not opinion problems. Match price to the *type* of value, extend the look-back window, and get in a boat people don't cancel.

- **Key frameworks:**
  - **Price-vs-value discrepancy** — value changes over time but a fixed price doesn't; people cancel when value drops to/below price. Keep value > price > cost (profit) with healthy "customer surplus" (goodwill).
  - **One-time vs consumable value** — a course/skill has *one-time* value (drops to zero once learned); community/support/hosting/ads have *recurring* value. Don't price recurring billing against one-time value — that's the churn engine.
  - **Big-head-long-tail pricing** — high one-time upfront fee (covers the one-time value + offsets CAC) + small recurring fee matched to ongoing consumable value. ($100M Offers concept.)
  - **Billing cadence kills/saves churn** — monthly ≈10.7% churn → quarterly ≈5% → annual ≈2%. LTV = price ÷ churn, so $100/mo goes from ~$950 LTV (monthly) to **$5,000 LTV (annual)** — a 5× business.
  - **The look-back window** — churn correlates with billing frequency. Customers judge "what have you done for me *since last bill*." Longer billing periods include the good months *and* the bad, so volatile-value services (ads/SEO) renew far better annually.
  - **Get in the right boat** — instead of fighting to reduce churn, sell things people *already* don't cancel (insurance, alarms, banking, phone) vs structurally cancel-prone (gyms).
  - **Raise price until conversion × price = less money** — it's about maximizing *revenue per conversation* (gross profit), not price itself. Test it; go low→high (easy), never high→low (painful).
  - **Push annual** — discount ~16% (buy-10-get-2), stack VIP status + bad-thing-removed (discount) + good-things-added (perks); annual front-loads a year of cash so you can spend aggressively on acquisition.
  - **Contracts ≈ worthless for consumers** (US chargeback culture); they only add front-end friction. They matter for Enterprise. Better to improve product quality + pick non-cancel boats.

- **Tactical takeaways:**
  1. Split your offer into one-time value (charge a setup fee) vs recurring value (smaller monthly).
  2. Move billing toward annual (or at least quarterly) — the LTV math alone can 5× the business.
  3. Report value *cumulatively* (Instacart "hours saved to date") to stretch the look-back window.
  4. Add a CPI clause to recurring contracts; raise prices every 2–3 years, infrequently.
  5. Test price low→high until revenue-per-conversation peaks, then back off one notch.

- **Best quote:** *"Most people stay poor because they think the only way to grow is to increase the number of customers they sell every month."*

- **Applies to Wollongong trades:** This is the blueprint for *your* pricing model. Charge a **one-time setup fee** (system install/onboarding — offsets your CAC and prices the one-time value) plus a **monthly recurring fee** for the ongoing speed-to-lead service. Then push tradies to **annual or quarterly billing** — your churn is the whole ballgame, and going annual could 5× your LTV per client. Extend the look-back window by sending a cumulative "jobs recovered & $ won since you started" report, so a slow month doesn't trigger a cancel. And you're already in a good "boat": missed-call → lost-job pain is structural and recurring, not a fad they cancel like a gym.

## Sales Was Hard Until I Understood These 9 Concepts
`https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cy2k1GdA-9o`

- **Core idea:** A salesperson has three jobs — **maximize opportunities, convert the highest %, and sustain it for a long time.** Every elite-seller behaviour maps to one of those three buckets. Sales is simply *increasing the likelihood the prospect buys*; a perfect process would convert 100%.

- **Key frameworks (the buckets + behaviours):**
  - **BUCKET 1 — Maximize opportunities:** (1) Most available hours/days = strongest predictor of total sales (availability beats everything; be free when the prospect is). (2) **Pull up calls** — drag later appointments into today (higher show + close); refill freed slots. (3) Two SOPs: an *on-call* script and an *off-call* checklist (most companies lack the latter and bleed money). (4) **BAMFAM** — "Book A Meeting From A Meeting," never end without the next step set. (5) Don't take rejection personally; keep a visible **kill list** of whales. (6) Create your own opportunities via **referrals** ("who do you know who's as awesome as you?").
  - **BUCKET 2 — Maximize conversion:** (7) **Preparation** — ~10% of call length in research; you sound like a genius and build instant rapport ("known → liked → trusted"). (8) **Take notes** → contextual recommendations turn you from salesperson into consultant. (9) **Listen 2× more than you talk** — best reps listen twice as much; "the one answering questions is the one being interrogated." Be like smoke; answer questions with questions unless you already know the answer aligns. (10) **Breathe the script** — memorize it cold so you're fully present; concision = more sales/day; rehearse one part at a time. (11) **Kill zombies up front** — handle *obstacles* (before price) early; *objections* (after price) are the ticking bomb. Concerns reduce to: circumstances (time/money/fit), other people (spouse/partner), or self-doubt. Use stories + past/future projection + **best-case/worst-case**. (12) **Ask for the sale again** — loop: resolve the concern, then re-ask, unlimited times. Whoever cares most about the prospect wins.
  - **BUCKET 3 — Meta-skills (sustain it):** (13) Consistency & enthusiasm are *skills*, not traits. **"Kill for sport"** — Mike's dogs/horses/tigers: be a tiger who'll close a low-value lead for the practice. **Track data** beyond close rate (booking %, show %, offer %, close %, avg call-to-close, cash collected). **Give the best leads to the best closers** (the timeshare rep who 5×'d a billion-dollar business). **Never blame circumstances** — take 100% control.

- **Tactical takeaways:**
  1. Expand availability (incl. weekends/evenings) and pull up appointments into open slots today.
  2. Build an explicit off-call checklist; BAMFAM every call.
  3. Spend ~10% of call length on prep; take notes; reference them next call.
  4. Flip the talk/listen ratio — answer questions with questions; memorize the script.
  5. Surface obstacles before quoting price; loop concern→re-ask until time runs out.
  6. Track the full funnel, not just close rate; route best leads to best closers.

- **Best quote:** *"Whoever cares the most about the prospect wins the sale... they don't feel sold, they feel like they bought."*

- **Applies to Wollongong trades:** This is your playbook for *both* selling your service AND the product you're selling. For your own outreach to tradies: maximize availability (sparkies are free early mornings/evenings), prep 5 min on each business (Google their reviews/site before the call), listen more than you pitch, and BAMFAM every conversation. For the **product**: your speed-to-lead system literally automates Bucket 1 for *them* — "pull up calls" and "maximize availability" become the instant text-back; never let a lead fall into no-man's-land. Pitch it as "we make sure every lead gets BAMFAM'd automatically — the #1 predictor of sales is just being available, and you're not."

## The Ultimate Sales Training for 2026 [Full Course]
`https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=StVqS0jD7Ls`

*Long-form course — structured by its 6 modules. Core thesis: sales = increasing the likelihood a prospect buys. A perfect process accounts for every variable and would convert 100% ("100 golden BBs"). Most leverage lives **outside** the call.*

### Module 1 — Sales Multipliers (things around the sale that beat anything said on it)
- **Sell 7 days/week** — off-hours (5pm+, weekends) are when consumers are free; +104 days/yr = **+29% revenue minimum**. Skipping weekends stacks no-shows onto Monday.
- **Respond in <1 minute** — contacting a lead within 60s = **391% higher** close likelihood; >5 min drops close odds ~80%. 50% of prospects buy from whoever responds *first*, not best. Add a "call now" option.
- **15-minute time slots** + an **off-call SOP** — more precise convenience lifts schedule + show rates. Faking scarcity (blocking slots) is dumb.
- **Pull up appointments** — drag later bookings into today (higher show + close); refill freed slots; qualify on the confirm call.
- **Feed the killers** — best leads → best closers (the timeshare rep who 5×'d a billion-dollar company); worst leads train new reps cheaply.
- **Shout out show-rate, not just close-rate** — a 20% show-rate lift = a 20% close-rate lift, and it's far easier to get.
- **Reminder cadence:** automated (look automated, don't fake-human) + manual personal touches at **24h / morning-of / 1h** (voice memo/video; show prep you've done).

### Module 2 — Sales Training
- **Role-play daily** — one correction at a time; trainability is inversely correlated with feedback speed; reinforce in real time ("great, keep going"), then drill the fixed line 3–6× in a row.
- **Best closer ≠ best manager** (Phil Jackson wasn't better than Jordan). Manager must have zero ego.
- **Compete vs *them*, not each other** — pick a common enemy ("every customer we don't close, they close").
- **Game-tape review with Sales + CS together** — record every call; aligns expectations, kills the sales↔CS conflict, arms closers with conviction. When *hot*, record and dissect it: "good closers try to get hot; great closers never get cold."
- **Conviction beats tone-drilling** — read testimonials to the team; kudos the rep who closed a now-successful customer. ("Weigh-out day" had the highest close rate because prospects saw results live.)
- **Sales = transferring conviction across a bridge of trust** — if the prospect knew everything you know, they'd buy; if they'd *not* buy knowing what you know, selling them is unethical.

### Module 3 — Before the Sale
- **The sale starts at the click** — setting script, ad, every touch frames the close.
- **Edify the closer** (Cialdini) — "you'll be with Shawn, he's helped 400 people just like you."
- **Qualify with BANT** — Budget, Authority, Need, Timing.
- **5 minutes of prep** = looking like a genius (everyone else is lazy); take notes → contextual = consultant not salesperson; never make the prospect repeat themselves.
- **Outbound gem:** "Hi, calling regarding [competitor]… call me back." Ghosted lead? Send the Kevin Hart "where you at" meme — highest re-engagement rate.

### Module 4 — Opening the Call
- **Proof → Promise → Plan** in ~15s (same as content openers); set the agenda → you control the frame. B2B: skip the foreplay, get to the point.
- **"Follow the f***ing script"** — script adherence is the FIRST thing to drill; if everyone follows it and no one closes, you have a *script* problem you can fix. First two-thirds = word-for-word; encode **tone/pacing/emphasis** into the script (underline/caps/italics). "I didn't say he hit his wife" — same words, 6 meanings.

### Module 5 — The CLOSER framework + closing
- **C**larify why they're here · **L**abel them with the problem · **O**verview past pain (the **pain cycle** — "the pain is the pitch"; deprivation → motivation) · **S**ell the vacation (the outcome, not the plane flight; **three-pillar pitch** ≤2 min + a metaphor per pillar, e.g. toothbrush/Amazing-Race for accountability) · **E**xplain away concerns (objection handling) · **R**einforce the decision (the **handshake**, not handoff — BAMFAM to CS).
- **Train back-to-front** — teach payment collection first (can't close without it). Closing begins when they say **no**; no ≠ no forever.
- **When they say yes — shut up.** Don't sell past the close.
- **Rapid-fire stall questions:** "What's your main concern?" → "What are you afraid of?" → "What variables are you using?" → "What would make this a yes / a no?"
- **All-purpose closes:** "Do you think the reason you're on this call is you've struggled to make this decision before?"; the **1–10 close** ("what would make it a 10? why not a 1?").
- **Details are death traps** — never give a "blind answer"; ask about the question, appeal to authority (**secretary/mechanic close** — "can't diagnose without looking under the hood"). A detail objection is really a *preference* objection ("you change the variables, you change the outcome").
- **AAA / 3A:** Acknowledge → Associate (foil: "someone earlier today…" / a successful customer / an authority) → Ask. Enables harsh truths without insult. **He who asks the most times closes the most** — rapport is what buys unlimited asks. Be like **smoke** (no one can disagree with a question); be **right or rich — pick rich**; childlike curiosity ("huh, tell me more").
- **Objection theory (understand, don't memorize):** Time = priorities; Decision-maker = support not permission; Details = preference; Money = value + resourcefulness-not-resources; Stalls = decision-avoidance (fear or not knowing how). **Stack closes** from different angles; **zoom out** when lost → **best-case/worst-case** (lethal with a guarantee).
- **Anti-guarantee** for no-guarantee offers; **ID close** (ask for ID before payment); **decision-maker 4-step** (do you actually have authority → afraid to tell me → they've already agreed via past behavior → support-not-permission).

### Module 6 — Mindset (reframed as behaviours)
- **Whoever cares most about the prospect wins** (qualify hard; turn people away; avoid "commission breath").
- **Volume negates luck** — skill↔volume flywheel; do 2× the best closer's effort.
- **Sales résumés don't matter** — group interviews > résumé screens; hire for the *smallest skill deficiency* (attitude vs aptitude).
- **Comp attracts closers, doesn't change behaviour** (except paying on *cash collected*); fast feedback loops drive behaviour.
- **Sales & marketing are one continuum** — bigger price = bigger plane = longer runway (more pre-education). Trust & belief are continuums, not binaries.
- **Say less** (let prospect talk ~2/3); **don't be cute** (do the fundamentals — "two fat guys run right"); **be a trash man** (yellow leads = gold practice); **people want to buy** — give them the excuse; **be kind, not nice**; **never negotiate price** ("we could do it for more" re-anchors) — change *terms*, not price.

- **Best quote:** *"You become rich by making them right, because they believe nothing about what you say and everything about what they say."*

- **Applies to Wollongong trades:** Modules 1 & 3 are *your entire product* — "<1-minute response = 391% higher close" and "50% buy from whoever responds first" is the literal ROI pitch for your speed-to-lead system; quote those stats verbatim to electricians. For selling the system yourself: prep 5 min on each tradie, open with Proof-Promise-Plan, run CLOSER, and use the foil close (you're young — "a sparky I spoke to last week…" beats "let me coach you"). The pain cycle is your discovery script: make them feel every missed-call-as-lost-job before you quote a price. And "feed the killers / trash man" — take *every* discovery call early to sharpen the pitch on hard prospects.

## How to Sell Better than 99% Of People (4 HOUR ULTIMATE GUIDE)
`https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JE2_7elAcxM`

*A compilation of Hormozi's best sales presentations (~4 hrs). Its CLOSER framework, sales multipliers, AAA/3A, tone/pacing/emphasis, and objection mechanics duplicate **The Ultimate Sales Training** entry above — refer there for those. Captured below is the one presentation unique to this video: **"Selling with Logic," plus its standalone closing frameworks.*

### Selling with Logic — the philosophical core (net-new)
- **Core idea:** Emotional buyers get excited and refund two days later; **logic is what makes a sale stick.** People *emotionally* want to buy — your job is to help their logical brain justify the decision they already want to make. Sales = "using logic to create an emotion." Channeling Munger: "we have a high moral responsibility to be rational."

- **21 beliefs about selling (the highlights):** selling happens *before* the ask, closing *after*; expect & plan for **no** (if they'd say yes on their own, you're not needed — "no is the job"); **selling is transferring belief across a bridge of trust** (you must *believe* to transfer it, and have *trust* to make the crossing); you go cold not by forgetting how to sell but by losing belief; **whoever cares most about the prospect wins** (including caring more than they do); **closers ask hard questions** because they genuinely care; **be kind, not nice**; "closing is a dance, not a fight — seduction, not war"; **power = the ability to direct/influence**; "if you didn't get a gasp from the price tag, you didn't go high enough."

- **The 3 distortions (Albert Ellis / CBT) — where prospects "cast their power":** every objection is one of three irrational distortions of reality:
  1. **Circumstances** — "I must get what I want / I can't stand it if I don't" → manifests as **Time**, **Money/Value**, **Fit**.
  2. **Others** — "people must treat me fairly" → manifests as **Authority** ("I have to ask my spouse/partner").
  3. **Self** — "I must do well or I'm no good" → manifests as **Avoidance/Stall** ("I need to think about it").
- **The onion of blame** — prospects start blaming the *outside* (circumstances), then *others*, then finally *self*. Each layer you peel tells you how close you are; once they say "I need to think about it," you're finally talking to the decision-maker in power.

- **Closes per distortion (understand the principle, don't memorize):**
  - **Time** → *macro* (busy season): "you'll be busy again — best to learn it now so it sticks forever"; *micro* (no time in the day): "a good program first cuts the 90% that isn't working"; *when-then fallacy*: "I'll pay for the thing that makes money once I have money" is a distortion.
  - **Money** → "is this a lot *to you*? — good, that's why you'll try hard"; relative vs absolute value; "you'll **pay either way — in time or money** (12 weeks or 12 years)"; **resourcefulness not resources** (you find money for an unexpected tax bill — be resourceful for yourself).
  - **Fit** → **new identity → new priorities** (the Sephora story); "you have to **change the change**"; the **hypothetical/1–10 close** ("if it were perfect, would you do it?").
  - **Authority** → sidestep the absent spouse ("what would they not like?"); "they don't approve of your current struggle — why'd they oppose fixing it?"; **support, not permission** (or you'll resent them in 5 years).
  - **Avoidance** → *past* ("don't let a bad past decision burn you twice"; "is the reason you're here that you've struggled to decide before?"); *present* ("decisions take **information, not time** — and I'm your only source"; the 3-question close: do you believe it'll work / do you trust me / will it work *for you*); *future* (cost-of-inaction; "another year of *almost*"); **decide = Latin "to cut off"** — which future are we killing?
  - **Universal close:** *"the reason you're telling yourself not to do this is the very reason you need to do it."*

- **The closing philosophy (the "invest in yourself" close):** "the #1 tax no one respects is the **time-tax of ignorance**"; "you buy time by buying the lessons other people's mistakes already paid for"; skills are the one asset "no government, no divorce can take from you" (his family fleeing Iran). Every decision is "a vote toward or against the person you want to become — Superman isn't coming, it's just you."

- **Best quote:** *"It doesn't take time to make decisions, it takes information — and I'm the only source of information you've got. So let's talk."*

- **Applies to Wollongong trades:** The 3-distortions map is a cheat-sheet for *every* objection a tradie throws at you. "I don't have time to learn another system" = Circumstances/Time → "the whole point is it saves you time by catching jobs you're already losing." "Money's tight right now" = "you're losing $X every missed call — you're paying for this either way, in lost jobs or in a subscription." "I need to think about it" = Avoidance → "what are you afraid of — that it won't book jobs? Let's look at the numbers now." And the **resourcefulness-not-resources** + **cost-of-inaction** frames are exactly how you reframe a sparky's "I can't afford it" into "you can't afford *not* to." Lead with logic — tradies are skeptical, concrete buyers; the ROI math closes them, not hype.
